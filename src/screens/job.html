On a job

Use the smallest safe next action. Never guess with power, flying, safety-critical equipment or a system you have not been authorised to alter. Open Job workspace →

Before the build

  1. Find the audio lead and confirm your task.
  2. Read the latest documents, not an old screenshot.
  3. Walk cable routes and power with the people who own them.
  4. Set up one clean work area; keep cases and walkways clear.

Open build checklist

When something is wrong

  1. Make it safe; mute or stop if needed.
  2. Ask: one thing or everything? What changed?
  3. Check the cheap, obvious points first.
  4. Trace one direction through the signal path.
  5. Escalate early with a clear update.

    People
    Being easy to work with is a technical skill. These cards explain who owns what, how to help, and when to stop and ask.
    
    
    
      
    Audio lead / A1
    They need
    Accurate prep, concise updates, no unapproved changes.
    You need
    Priorities, a patch plan and permission before altering the system.
    Say
    “I’ve checked X and Y. The fault is now between A and B. Do you want me to swap the cable?”
    
      
    Warehouse & prep
    They need
    Returned kit counted, labelled and quarantined when faulty—not hidden back in stock.
    You need
    The current pull list, substitutions and the company’s labelling system.
    Say
    “This is intermittent under movement; I’ve labelled it and left it out of service.”
    
      
    Production / venue
    They need
    Early, factual risks—not last-minute panic or jargon.
    You need
    Schedule, access, client changes, house rules and decision makers.
    Say
    “This needs a decision before doors: we can do A now, or B with more time.”
    
      
    Lighting, video & staging
    They need
    Clear shared routes, respect for truss/power boundaries and no surprise repatching.
    You need
    Agreement before sharing space, power, network or comms.
    Say
    “Before I run this, is this route clear for your cable and access?”
    
      
    Artists, presenters & stage management
    They need
    Calm, practical help and no technical monologue when they are under pressure.
    You need
    Changes, requirements and a route through the right person.
    Say
    “I’m going to check that with the audio lead and come straight back to you.”
    
      
    Rigging, power & safety leads
    They need
    You to respect exclusion zones, stop instructions and authorisation limits.
    You need
    Safe access, approved positions and clear ownership.
    Say
    “I’m not authorised to change that. Who owns this system?”
    
    
    
  
    
  
    
    
    Quick guides for the job itself
    Open these when the job is familiar enough to need a prompt, not a textbook.
    
    
    The d&b rack — the short version
    1. Get the plan: box, amp, channel, output mode, setup.
    2. Test every speaker cable at prep; 4-core wherever the plan uses Mix TOP/SUB or 2-Way Active.
    3. Amps close to the boxes they drive; powerCON locked.
    4. Speaker cables into the socket the mode feeds, twisted until they click, labelled both ends.
    5. Power up last, channels muted.
    6. Check the setup on every channel against the box it's actually driving.
    7. R1 online, every amp showing; System check if you have it.
    8. Prove it one box at a time at low level, then hand over to the system tech.
    The full twelve steps are in §6.18, output modes and pins in §6.17, and if anything misbehaves, talk it through.
    
    
    End-of-shift learning log — three minutes
    1. Write the job type and your role, with no confidential client detail.
    2. Record one item you handled and what it does.
    3. Record one issue: symptom, what changed, checks made, who resolved it.
    4. Write one question to ask the right person on the next shift.
    5. Choose one five-to-ten-minute topic in this handbook before the next job.
    
    
    
    How to learn an unfamiliar piece of kit at work
    1. Ask whether you may explore it and when—not during a pressured build.
    2. Find the exact model and open its official manual.
    3. Identify inputs, outputs, power, network/control and normal status lights.
    4. Follow one safe, reversible task with an experienced person nearby.
    5. Write a small “what this box does here” note; never save over a live show file.
    
    
    How to ask for the equipment-system information
    Ask the person who owns warehouse systems rather than exporting data yourself:
    1. “I’m building a private learning app for my audio development. May I have a read-only list or screenshots of the labels and categories I am allowed to use?”
    2. Ask specifically for the fields TreebySound needs: stock name, manufacturer/model, internal label/barcode, category, case or shelf location, connector/cable type, prep notes, service status and hire-in flag.
    3. Confirm what must not be copied: client data, supplier prices, serial numbers, access credentials, job contacts or live availability.
    4. Start with one small category—microphones, DIs or common cable types—and compare the export against physical labels with a supervisor.
    5. Only then decide whether to import a cleaned reference list or simply link out to the company system. Keeping one source of truth is more important than copying everything.
