PART 20

Words & terms

Every piece of jargon in one place. Search it, or browse it by area if you know the topic but not the word.

20.1 Full A–Z

Each term links to where it's properly explained, plus the other places it actually matters — treat this as an index as much as a glossary. Multiple links are ordered: definition first, then practical/fault-finding uses. Prefer to browse by topic — jump to §20.2 instead.

Absorption
How soft surfaces — drapes, seating and above all an audience — convert sound energy to heat instead of reflecting it. Why a full room sounds drier than an empty one. §6.15
ABTT
Association of British Theatre Technicians — the theatre world's recognised training body. §17.4 · §10.4
ADAT
An older optical ("lightpipe") digital audio format carrying 8 channels at 48 kHz. Legacy, but still common in studios and small rigs. §3.7
Advancing
Finding out what a job actually involves before anyone loads a van — dates, power, access, what's already in the room. §1.7 · §19.9
AEC
Acoustic echo cancellation — processing that stops remote call participants hearing their own voice echo back through the room. §10.8
AES3 / AES-EBU
The AES standard for carrying two channels of digital audio down a single (110 Ω) XLR cable — looks exactly like a normal mic cable but isn't. §3.7
AES50
A simple point-to-point digital audio protocol over Cat5e, used by Behringer/Midas X32/M32/Wing for their stageboxes. Fixed and cheap, but not a routable network like Dante. §3.7
AES67
An interoperability standard letting Dante and other compatible systems exchange audio directly, often at the broadcast boundary. §8.2 · §14.5
AFL
After-Fade Listen — solo mode that reflects the channel's fader position. §7.6
A Greener Future
The sustainability certification body for events (formerly A Greener Festival), auditing against environmental standards including power supply and generator fuel. §3.8
AI-assisted tools (automixing / feedback suppression)
Emerging products that automatically gain-share open mics or detect and notch ringing frequencies. Worth knowing about, not a substitute for understanding gain structure or ringing out a wedge yourself. §7.3 · §15.8
Ambient mic
A mic used to blend a sense of the room into a sealed IEM mix. §15.4
Amplified controller
L-Acoustics' name for an amp with built-in processing and loudspeaker presets (LA4X, LA12X, LA7.16). §6.21
Arc / Line / AP (d&b setups)
Variants of a line-array box's setup: Arc for the curved part of a hang, Line for the straight part, AP when ArrayProcessing is in use. The plan says which box gets which. §6.20
ArrayCalc
d&b's free system design software: models the room and the boxes, works out angles, rigging loads and delays, and produces the amp setups and an R1 project. §6.7 · §6.16
ArrayProcessing
A d&b ArrayCalc option that calculates individual filters for every box in a line array to shape coverage and tone front to back. Never tweaked by hand. §6.16
ATA Carnet
An international customs document letting professional equipment cross a border temporarily without import duty, on the promise it leaves again. §17.8
Audio description
A live spoken narration of a show's visual action for blind and partially-sighted audience members, delivered by a describer over a dedicated feed. §6.13
Audio lead / A1
The person responsible for the audio system or show mix on that job. Their exact title varies; establish who has authority before changing a live system. People · §1.3
AudioMoth
A low-cost, open-source acoustic recorder used for passive acoustic monitoring; full-spectrum versions record ultrasound, so they cover bats as well as birds. §18.15
Automixer
Automatically shares gain between speech mics so the total stays steady however many people talk — e.g. the Dugan automixer on a DM7. §7.8 · §10.11
Aux send
A separate feed from a channel to somewhere else — a wedge, an IEM mix, an effect. §7.1 · §15.3 · §16.5
AVIXA CTS
Certified Technology Specialist — the standard credential in corporate and permanent-install AV. §17.4 · §10.2 · §10.6
AvoKey
The USB licence dongle that unlocks full Avolites Titan software. §9.8 · §9.9 · §18.10
Backline
A band's own amps, drums and instrument rigs. §9.4
Balanced
A connection carrying the signal twice (normal and inverted) plus a shield, cancelling picked-up interference. §2.3 · §3.1
BAPAM
The British Association for Performing Arts Medicine — a UK charity supporting the health of people working in live events and performance, crew included. §1.10
Bectu
The UK entertainment and broadcast trade union (a sector of Prospect). §17.4
Beltpack (comms)
The pack a crew member wears for intercom, with a headset, talk button and channel selection. §8.12 · §11.8
Bioacoustics
The study of sound produced by and affecting living organisms — the field where live-audio recording skills transfer directly into ecology. Outside work · §18.15
BirdNET
A free machine-learning classifier from the Cornell Lab that identifies species from recordings; commonly paired with Raven Pro for spectrogram analysis. §18.15
Bit depth
How precisely each digital audio sample is measured — 24-bit is the live standard. §2.9 · §14.4
Blacks
Plain black working clothes worn on stage from soundcheck onwards, and always in theatre, so crew don’t catch the audience’s eye. §1.6
Broadcast submix (TX mix)
A separate, drier mix built for a broadcast or stream feed rather than for the room. §14.5 · §14.8
BS 7909
The industry code of practice for temporary electrical systems at events. §3.8 · §13.2 · §18.8
Bus / mix bus
A shared signal path that more than one channel is summed into — an aux, group/DCA, the matrix, or the main mix itself. §16.10 · §7.3
Call sheet
A personalised day-of document — your own call time, site address, parking and contacts — distinct from the show's overall timing schedule. §17.2
Call time
The time you are required to be on site and ready to work — not the time you set off. §1.7 · §1.4
CCL (d&b CL-Series)
d&b's compact cardioid line array (2025): CCL8, CCL12 and CCL-SUB, cardioid from a passive box on one amp channel. §6.20
CDM 2015
UK construction safety regulations that apply to event builds and de-rigs. §13.1 · §13.6
ceeform
The round industrial power connector family used across UK events — blue 16 A and 32 A for single-phase, red for three-phase. §3.8
CEE form
Industrial power connectors: blue for single-phase 230 V (16, 32, 63 A), red for three-phase 400 V. §3.8
Changeover
The gap between acts where one band's setup is struck and the next built. §10.3
Channel 38
UHF 606.5–613.5 MHz — the UK-wide shared radio mic licence band; shared and unprotected, so still scan. §5.4 · §5.12
Channel 70
UHF 863.1–864.9 MHz — licence-exempt radio mic band in the UK; free but crowded. §5.12
Chroma key
Compositing one video source over another by removing a specific colour (usually green) to reveal what's behind it. §9.14
CIEEM / Chartered Ecologist (CEcol)
The Chartered Institute of Ecology and Environmental Management — the UK professional body for ecologists, and the chartered status that shows continued professional practice. §18.15
Click track
A metronome feed, usually to the drummer's IEMs, that the band plays to. §15.10
Clock leader
The one device on a digital audio network that every other device takes its timing from. Two leaders cause clicks and drop-outs. §8.9 · §2.9
Clock / word clock
The shared timing reference every device on a digital audio network must agree on — "word clock" is the term actually printed on converters and consoles for it. §2.9 · §8.2 · §12.14
Coherence
In measurement software, how trustworthy a reading is at each frequency — low coherence means reflections or noise. §8.11
Compressor
Reduces dynamic range by turning down anything above a threshold. §16.4 · §7.5
Console redundancy / hot-spare
A second console mirroring or ready to take over from the primary if it fails — full real-time redundancy on bigger shows, a manually-loaded spare on smaller ones. §7.4
COSHH
Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 — covers anything on site that could harm you if inhaled, touched or swallowed, from haze fluid to cleaning solvents. §13.14
CPL (d&b)
A d&b channel function that compensates for boxes coupling together at low frequencies in an array. §6.17
Crew Chief
Runs the audio crew on site day to day — your boss for the day. §1.3
Critical distance
The distance from a loudspeaker at which the reverberant field is as loud as the direct sound; beyond it, intelligibility falls away however loud you go. §6.15
Cue list (lighting)
A sequence of recorded lighting cues triggered in order from a playback fader — Titan's equivalent of a console scene sequence. §9.10
Cue-to-cue
A theatre technical rehearsal that skips the dialogue between cues and runs only the cue points themselves, to drill timing and calls without running the whole show each time. §19.19
Curfew
The legally binding time by which sound must stop, usually a condition of the premises or event licence rather than a courtesy. §1.5 · §6.10
Custom-moulded IEM
An earpiece cast from an impression of the performer's own ear canal, for a better seal and more consistent low end than a universal-fit tip. §15.12
CUT (d&b)
A d&b channel function: a high-pass that takes the low end out of a top when it runs with subs. §6.17
Dante
A protocol for moving multichannel digital audio over standard Ethernet networks. §8.2 · §12.14 · §18.3
Dante Virtual Soundcard
Software that turns a computer into a Dante device for multitrack recording and playback. §14.7 · §7.9
dBA / dBC
Weighted sound-pressure measurements: A-weighting discounts bass heavily and is what most noise conditions and hearing-exposure limits are written in; C-weighting is much flatter and better reflects what actually annoys neighbours. §2.10 · §6.10
d&b (d&b audiotechnik)
German loudspeaker and amplifier maker common on UK touring, theatre and corporate work. A d&b system is the box, a d&b amp and the matching loudspeaker setup, used together. §6.16 · §6.18 · §6.20
dBFS
Decibels relative to digital full scale — 0 dBFS is the absolute digital ceiling. §2.10 · §2.5
d&b loudspeaker setup
The preset chosen on each d&b amp channel for the exact box it drives — crossover, EQ and the protection that keeps the drivers alive. The wrong one is the most expensive mistake on a d&b rig. §6.16 · §6.17
d&b output mode
How a d&b amp pairs its channels — Dual Channel, Mix TOP/SUB or 2-Way Active — which decides which output socket to use and which NL4 pins carry what. §6.17
dB SPL
Decibels of actual sound pressure in the air — what a sound level meter reads. §2.10 · §13.5
dBu
Decibels referenced to 0.775 V — the analogue pro-audio line-level unit. "+4 dBu" is standard pro line level. §2.10
dBV
Decibels referenced to 1 V — the consumer line-level unit, e.g. "−10 dBV". Quieter than pro dBu level. §2.10
DCA / VCA
A master fader that remotely controls a group of channel faders without the audio passing through it — “all vocals” on one fader. §7.3
De-esser
A compressor that only engages on sibilant ("s", "sh") frequencies, taming harsh esses without dulling the rest of the vocal. §16.9
Delay (effect)
A discrete echo effect, from a short slapback to a longer tempo-synced repeat — distinct from reverb and from acoustic feedback. §16.8
Delay speaker
A speaker further back, time-aligned to the main PA so the audience hears one system, set quieter so the main still feels like the source. §6.6 · §6.8
Derig
Taking the whole show down after it's finished. §1.2 · §19.7
DI box
Direct Injection box — converts unbalanced, high-impedance signals to balanced, low-impedance ones. §3.9 · §12.10
Direct sound
The sound arriving straight from the loudspeaker before any reflections — what carries intelligibility. §6.15
DISPLAY
Martin Audio's prediction and optimisation software for designing Wavefront Precision arrays. §6.23
Diversity (true diversity)
A wireless receiver with two antennas and two receiver sections, continuously using whichever has the better signal, to defeat multipath dropouts. §5.3
DMX
The digital control protocol lighting fixtures use — not audio. §9.7 · §9.10 · §12.16
Doors
The moment the audience is let into the room — after which everything on stage stops. §1.4
DSM (Deputy Stage Manager)
The theatre role that actually calls a show — "standby… go" — cueing sound, lighting and stage moves in real time under the Stage Manager. §10.4 · §18.13
DSP
Digital Signal Processor/Processing — the EQ, crossover, delay and dynamics engine built into modern amps, install-audio boxes and conferencing hardware, replacing racks of separate outboard units. §6.4 · §10.6
Dugan
See Automixer — Dan Dugan's automatic speech-mixing system, built into the DM7. §7.8
Earth / ground loop
Hum caused by two devices referencing mains earth at different physical points. §12.3 · §3.8 · §12.10
EBU R128
The European broadcast loudness recommendation, targeting −23 LUFS integrated — a different (quieter) target than streaming platforms use. §14.6
EEE (Energy Efficient Ethernet)
A switch power-saving mode (802.3az), often called Green Ethernet, that can add enough latency and jitter to a port to cause audible clicks on a Dante network — recommended off on any Dante-carrying port. §8.2
Electricity at Work Regulations 1989
The UK regulation requiring electrical equipment to be maintained safely — the legal root of PAT testing. §13.2 · §12.15
EMF / RF exposure
Electromagnetic field exposure from RF transmitters, regulated in the UK by the Control of Electromagnetic Fields at Work Regulations 2016 — a real but low day-to-day risk at normal working distances. §5.10
Equal-loudness contours
The measured fact that the ear’s frequency response changes with level — less bass is heard quietly — which is why a mix built at one volume does not translate to another. §16.7
Equipment insurance
A separate freelancer policy from PLI, covering loss, theft or damage to kit you personally own. §17.2 · §17.5
Escalation
Handing a problem to the person authorised or experienced enough to own it, with a concise account of the symptom, what changed and what has been checked. It is a safe professional action, not a failure. On a job · §12.7
etherCON
Neutrik's ruggedised, locking housing around a standard RJ45 network connector — survives being trodden on and is the standard choice for network cable on site rather than a bare RJ45. §3.6
Fatigue (crew)
Tiredness treated as a safety risk rather than a personal weakness: it causes misjudged lifts, mis-patches and dangerous drives home. §13.9
Feedback
A runaway audio loop between a microphone and a loudspeaker it can hear, heard as a rising howl or ring; fixed by reducing gain first and cutting the ringing frequency second. §12.18 · §15.8
Fill speaker
A speaker covering an area the main PA misses — front fill, out-fill, in-fill. §6.6
Fixture personality
The definition file that tells Titan what a lighting fixture's DMX channels actually do. §9.10
Flexi Channel (DiGiCo)
DiGiCo's reconfigurable channel-strip architecture, letting the order of gate, EQ and dynamics be changed per channel rather than fixed as on most desks. §7.1
FMOD
Firelight Technologies' game-audio middleware — connects sound assets to a game engine's logic. Runs its own free learning courses. §21.5
Focus Recall
On Yamaha desks, a per-scene setting for what that scene is allowed to change. §7.8 · §7.4
FOH (front of house)
Front of House — both the mix position out in the room and the engineer who works there, mixing what the audience hears, as opposed to monitor world. §1.3 · §15.1
Gain compensation
A feature on split digital console systems that automatically applies an equal, opposite digital trim on the other console when one engineer changes the shared analogue preamp gain, so neither console's level jumps unexpectedly. §7.3
Gain / trim
The input control that sets how much the preamp amplifies an incoming signal. §2.4 · §12.9 · §12.12
GALAXY
Meyer Sound's system processor for routing, alignment and EQ of arrays. §6.23
Gate
Mutes a channel below a set threshold, commonly used to control drum bleed. §16.3
Genlock
A sync signal keeping multiple video sources changing frames at the same instant, so switching between them doesn't glitch — the video equivalent of word clock. §9.13
Get-in / get-out
The start and end of load-in and derig on site — theatre's words for the same jobs covered in §1.2, different vocabulary depending on the building you're in. §19.4 · §10.4
Gooseneck
A flexible-stemmed condenser mic for lecterns and tables. §4.9 · §4.4
gPTP
The timing protocol AVB/Milan switches use to keep every device in step. §8.10
Grand Master (lighting)
The Titan fader controlling overall output intensity for the whole lighting rig — distinct from a console's audio master. §9.10
Graphic EQ (GEQ)
Fixed-band EQ, usually 31 bands, adjusted with sliders — fast and visual. Traditional on monitor wedges and system outputs. §7.5 · §6.9
Ground lift
A switch on a DI box (or an isolation transformer) that disconnects the audio shield to break a ground loop, while leaving the mains safety earth fully intact. Off by default; only switched on to fix a hum. §3.9 · §12.3 · §12.10
Handover
A clear transfer of information or responsibility between people or shifts: what is working, what changed, what remains to do, and any risk. People · §1.5
Headroom
The space between a normal working level and the point of clipping. §2.5 · §14.2
Headset mic
A mic on a boom from an ear-hook, capsule at the corner of the mouth — best for presenters who move or speak quietly. §10.11 · §4.9
Hearing loop (induction loop)
A wire loop and driver amplifier feeding a magnetic field that a hearing aid's T-coil picks up directly. Tested to IEC 60118-4. §6.11 · §18.11
Heritage (d&b)
d&b's name for discontinued products — J-Series, Q-Series, D6, D12, D20 and others — which are still widely used in hire stock. §6.20
HFA (d&b)
A d&b channel function giving a gentle high-frequency roll-off for listeners close to the box. §6.17
Hire-in
Equipment sourced from another supplier for a particular job rather than from the company’s own stock. Confirm the exact model, version, ownership, return condition and authorised changes before treating it like familiar kit. Job workspace
Hi-vis
High-visibility vest or jacket, required during build and derig on many sites — and deliberately off stage once doors are open. §1.4
House tech (venue tech)
The venue’s own technician — knows where the power, tie lines and noise limiter are. Worth being nice to. §1.3
HPF
High-pass filter — removes everything below a set frequency. §7.1
HVO
Hydrotreated vegetable oil — a lower-carbon, drop-in alternative to red diesel for generators. §3.8
Hybrid meeting (video conferencing)
A meeting with both an in-room and a remote audience, needing the room to hear and be heard both ways. §10.8
IEM
In-ear monitor — a sealed personal monitor mix delivered wirelessly or by cable. §5.7 · §15.2 · §15.3
IGMP querier
The device on a network responsible for actively tracking multicast group membership so IGMP snooping works correctly — needed alongside snooping, not instead of it, on any switch handling more than a few Dante devices. §8.2
Immersive audio (object-based)
Systems like L-Acoustics L-ISA or d&b Soundscape that place and move sound precisely across many individually-controlled boxes. §6.6
Impedance
Resistance to an AC signal, measured in ohms — governs how sources and inputs should be matched. §2.7 · §3.9
Input list
The numbered list of every audio source on a show, with mic type and power needs. §1.5 · §11.2
Insert (point)
A patch point in a channel's processing chain — physical on an analogue console, a menu option on a digital one — where an external processor can be dropped in instead of, or alongside, the built-in EQ and dynamics. §16.10
Institute of Acoustics (IOA)
The UK professional body for acoustics. Its part-time Diploma in Acoustics and Noise Control is a work-alongside route into acoustics consultancy that doesn't require a full degree restart. §21.10
Intermodulation (intermod)
Unwanted frequencies produced when two or more transmitters mix in a receiver’s front end, which can land on a channel you’re using. Why coordinated frequency groups exist. §5.5
Interpretation system
Simultaneous-translation equipment — booths, wireless IR/RF receivers — usually fed a clean feed of the source language by audio. §6.12
IP rating
The two-digit Ingress Protection code (IP54, IP55, IP65…) on outdoor-rated gear, describing resistance to dust and water. §6.14
iRecord / NBN Atlas
The UK route for submitting verifiable biological records and the national dataset they feed, making an observation usable evidence rather than a private note. §18.15
ISP / GR / OVL
The channel indicators on a d&b amp: ISP (green) input signal present; GR (yellow) gain reduction — the limiter is working; OVL (red) overload, or on touchscreen amps a muted channel. §6.17 · §6.19
Job pack
The collection of approved documents for a job: usually schedule, kit list, input list, stage plan, contacts and RAMS where applicable. Treat the latest company-held version as the source of truth. Job workspace · §1.5
Kit list
What's actually going on the job — the warehouse-facing document. §1.5 · §19.2
Latency
The delay introduced by converting, processing and transporting a signal. §2.9 · §8.2
Lavalier
A tiny clip-on mic, worn on the chest for speech or at the hairline for theatre. §10.11 · §4.9
Learning log
A short private record of a job, skill, question and lesson. Keep it factual and free of client-sensitive information; it turns experience into evidence of progress. Quick guides
Leq
Equivalent continuous level — a noise limit averaged over a stated period (e.g. "Leq 15 min"), rather than an instantaneous peak. How most noise conditions are actually written. §6.10
Licensing Act 2003
The UK legislation under which premises licences and Temporary Event Notices are granted — noise limits and curfews are usually attached to these as legal conditions. §6.10
Limited company
A separate legal business structure some freelancers move to once turnover justifies it, distinct from trading as a sole trader. §17.2
Limiter
A compressor with a very high ratio, used to stop a signal exceeding a hard ceiling — protective on outputs, essential on IEMs. §7.5 · §5.7
Line array
A vertical column of matched loudspeaker elements flown together, designed to control vertical coverage over long throw distances. §6.2
Line check
Proving every individual input works correctly before a show. Part 11 · §19.5
Load-in / load-out
Getting the kit into the venue at the start, and back onto the truck at the end — steps 3 and 7 of the hire lifecycle. §1.2 · §19.3
LoadMatch
A feature of current d&b amps that electrically compensates for the speaker cable, so a box at the end of a long run sounds like one on a short run. Needs the real cable length and size entered. §6.16
LOLER
UK regulations governing the inspection of lifting equipment. §13.3
Lone working
Doing a task without anyone else present or nearby — changes what counts as a safe way to do it. §13.12
LTC
Linear timecode — an audio-rate sync signal used to lock playback to video. §9.3
LUFS
A loudness measurement standard used for broadcast and streaming normalisation. §14.6
MADI
A simple, fixed 56/64-channel digital audio link over one coax or fibre cable, with no network configuration. §8.2
Manual handling
The regulations and practical habits around lifting and moving things safely by hand. §13.4
MAPP 3D
Meyer Sound's system design and prediction software. §6.23
Martyn's Law
The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 — venues and events must prepare for an attack; expected in force spring 2027. §13.16
Matrix
A mixer of mixes — takes existing buses and builds extra outputs from them, for delay stacks, fills, broadcast or the foyer. §7.3
Metadata (field recording)
The contextual information attached to an observation or recording: date, time, place, habitat, weather, equipment and identification confidence. Good metadata makes a conservation record useful. Outside work
MEWP
Mobile Elevating Work Platform — a "cherry picker" or scissor lift used to fly PA or work at height. A trained, ticketed role; not something to have a go at unsupervised. §13.1 · §13.3
Mic level
The very small voltage produced directly by a microphone, needing a preamp. §2.2
Milan (AVB)
An open network audio standard used natively by d&b, L-Acoustics and Meyer — a separate ecosystem from Dante, not a variant of it. §8.2
Mix-minus
A feed of everything except the receiver's own signal — so a remote caller doesn't hear themselves back. §10.8 · §14.14
MixRack
The Allen & Heath dLive box holding the processing and most of the I/O; the Surface is the control desk. §7.12
Mix TOP/SUB
A d&b output mode that sends a top (pins 1±) and a sub (pins 2±) down one 4-core NL4 cable from the centre A/B or C/D output. §6.17 · §6.19
MTC
MIDI timecode — a data-based sync signal video systems can send to lock playback, distinct from the audio-rate LTC. §9.3
Multicore
A single cable bundling many audio channels together — also called a snake. §3.5 · §9.5
Multipath
The same RF signal arriving by two different paths and cancelling itself at one point in space — the usual cause of a wireless dropout at one specific spot on stage. True diversity defeats it. §5.3 · §5.9
Music Producers Guild (MPG)
The UK trade body for studio and record-production engineers. §21.4
Mute group
A key that mutes several channels at once — check it first when a whole section goes quiet. §7.3 · §7.7
NDI
Video carried over a standard network switch instead of dedicated cable — the video equivalent of Dante. §9.13
NEBOSH General Certificate
A widely-recognised health and safety qualification, the usual next step up from IOSH Working Safely. §21.10
Nebra
Meyer Sound software that monitors every self-powered box: amp voltage, limiting, temperature, drivers. §6.23
Network isolation
Keeping a production audio network physically or logically separate from a venue's guest WiFi or public internet, so it can't be seen or interfered with from outside. §8.6
NL4
Four-pole speakON: pins 1+/1− and 2+/2−, so two amp channels can share one cable. The everyday speaker connector on d&b and most passive systems. §3.3 · §3.12
NL8
The eight-pole speakON: four amp channels down one multicore. §3.12 · §6.17
NLT4 (STX)
Neutrik's heavy-duty, all-metal STX version of the NL4 cable connector — same pins and socket, tougher and higher-rated. What "NLT4" means on a touring cable label. §3.12
Noise at Work (exposure action values)
The UK regulations protecting your own hearing over a career — 80/85/87 dB(A). §13.5
Open Sound Meter
Free measurement software for comparing what goes into a system with what a measurement mic hears. §8.11
Opt-out (48-hour)
A voluntary written agreement to work more than an average 48 hours a week; cancellable with 7 days' notice (up to 3 months if agreed). §13.15
Oscillator
The desk's built-in tone or pink-noise generator — for checking outputs and following a signal. Start low. §7.6 · §12.19
OSC (Open Sound Control)
A lightweight open protocol for sending cues and control messages over IP between different systems — lighting, show control, consoles. Not an audio-transport protocol. §8.6 · §10.4 · §9.8
PA
Public Address — the loudspeaker system delivering sound to the audience. Part 6 · §11.1 · §12.13
Pad
A fixed attenuator for very hot sources, applied before the preamp. §7.1
Palette (lighting)
A saved, reusable Titan setting (colour, position or beam) that cues reference rather than copy. §9.10
Pan
A channel's position in the stereo image. §7.1
Parametric EQ (PEQ)
EQ with adjustable frequency, gain and bandwidth (Q) per band — surgical, and the main working tool on a channel. §7.5 · §15.8
Partyline
Wired intercom where belt packs share a line powered from a main station — e.g. Clear-Com Encore. §8.12
Passive acoustic monitoring (PAM)
Surveying wildlife by leaving autonomous recorders in a habitat and analysing what was captured, rather than observing in person. Outside work · §18.15
Patch list
Where each input physically lands — stagebox, socket, console channel. §1.5 · §11.2
PAT testing
Portable Appliance Testing — one common method of meeting electrical safety duties, with no fixed legal interval. §13.8
PFL
Pre-Fade Listen — solo mode that ignores the channel's fader position. §7.6
Phantom power
The +48 V DC supply sent up a mic cable to power condenser mics and active DIs. §2.6 · §12.9
Phase
A frequency-dependent time relationship between two signals, distinct from polarity. §2.8 · §12.6
Pink noise
Noise with equal energy per octave — the standard test signal for checking and measuring speakers. §12.19 · §8.11
Pixel pitch
The distance between individual LEDs on a video wall, in millimetres — a smaller number gives a sharper image at close range. §9.15
PLASA (NELT)
The UK entertainment technology trade body; National Event Lifting Training is its accessible entry point into rigging. §17.4 · §18.12
PLI
Public Liability Insurance — covers a freelancer if their work injures someone or damages property. §17.2
PMR446
The licence-exempt short-range walkie-talkie band around 446 MHz — a common uncoordinated RF neighbour on site. §5.9
PMSE
Programme Making and Special Events — Ofcom's category for the spectrum wireless mics and IEMs use. PMSE rules change; always check the current Ofcom guidance rather than assuming a band is still available. §5.4 · §18.5
Point source
A speaker that radiates from a single point and drops off faster with distance than a line array. §6.2 · §6.6
Polarity
Whether a waveform is the right way up — flipping it inverts the whole signal instantly. §2.8 · §11.3 · §12.10
Polar pattern
The shape of a microphone's sensitivity to sound arriving from different directions. §4.2
powerCON TRUE1
A locking mains connector rated to connect and disconnect under load (unlike the older blue/grey powerCON). §3.8
PPE
Personal protective equipment — steel toes, hi-vis, hearing protection, gloves. What's required varies by site and task. §13.11
Preferred Leader
The Dante Controller setting that makes one device the clock leader — set on that one device only. §8.9
Preset (L-Acoustics)
L-Acoustics' equivalent of a d&b setup: the box-specific processing loaded into the amplified controller. Suffixes: _FI fill, _MO monitor. §6.21
Programmer (lighting)
Titan's temporary workspace holding fixture changes until they're recorded into a cue or cleared. §9.10
PRS for Music / PPL
UK music licensing bodies — PRS covers the songwriter/composer side, PPL the recording/performer side. Recording or streaming copyrighted music can need permission beyond a venue's standard performance licence. §14.11
PSA
Production Services Association — the trade body built around the live-event crew role, with a competency card scheme and apprenticeship route. §17.4 · §18.12
Purple Guide
The HSE-endorsed, industry-written reference for health, safety and welfare at music and other events. §13.1 · §18.8
PUWER
UK regulations governing the safe provision and use of work equipment generally. §13.3
QLab
Mac cue-based playback and show-control software — the theatre and events standard for firing audio, video and lighting cues in sequence from a spacebar. Free to use; paid licences unlock more simultaneous output channels. §8.4 · §10.4 · §18.13
QoS (Quality of Service)
Switch-level priority tagging that lets urgent traffic — Dante's clock and audio packets — jump the queue ahead of everything else sharing the same network. §8.2
Quarantine (equipment)
Clearly separating faulty or suspect kit from working stock so it cannot return to a job by accident. Label the symptom and report it through the company process. People · §11.5
R1 (d&b Remote)
d&b's control software: shows and controls every amp on the network — setups, levels, mutes, delays and live meters. Control only; the amps pass audio without it. §6.16 · §6.19
RAMS
Risk Assessment & Method Statement — the standard safety paperwork for a task with meaningful risk. §13.7 · §1.5
RCD
Residual Current Device — trips on an earth leakage imbalance to protect against electric shock, a different failure mode from an overcurrent breaker or fuse. §3.8 · §12.15
RDM (Remote Device Management)
A bidirectional extension of DMX512 that lets a compatible console discover, monitor and remotely address fixtures over the same cable. §9.7
Recall filter
On Allen & Heath desks, stops a scene changing chosen channels or parameters; global safes protect from every scene. §7.12
Recall Safe
On Yamaha desks, protects chosen channels or parameters from every scene recall. §7.8 · §7.4
Recall scope
On DiGiCo desks, what each snapshot is allowed to change — the classic first-time trap. §7.11
Redline
Marking up the plan (stage plan, patch list, input list) with what was actually done on site, not just what was originally planned — handed over so the next crew or advance isn't working from stale paperwork. §1.5
Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005
The fire safety law for non-domestic premises in England and Wales — Scotland and Northern Ireland use separate legislation. §13.10
Reverberation time (RT60)
How long sound takes to decay by 60 dB after the source stops — the single most useful number for how live or dead a room is. §6.15
RF
Radio frequency — the world of wireless mics and IEM transmission. Part 5 · §12.11 · §18.5
RIDDOR
Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 — the law behind reporting accidents and near-misses. §13.13
Rider
An artist's or client's requested technical needs — aspirational rather than contractual. §1.5
Ringing out
Deliberately finding and cutting a monitor's feedback-prone frequencies before a mic is anywhere near it. §6.9 · §15.8
Rio
Yamaha's Dante stage boxes; the desk controls their preamps (HA remote). §7.8
Room mode (standing wave)
A low-frequency resonance set by a room’s dimensions, making bass strong in some positions and absent in others with nothing wrong with the system. §6.15
RTA
Real Time Analyser — shows frequency content live. Useful for spotting a feedback or problem frequency fast; not a substitute for listening. §7.5 · §8.3 · §15.8
Safety Advisory Group (SAG)
The local authority, police, fire and other agencies who jointly review a bigger or higher-risk event and can set or tighten licence conditions such as noise limits. §6.10
Sample rate
How many times per second a digital audio signal is measured — 48 kHz is the live standard. §2.9 · §14.4 · §12.14
Scene / snapshot
A stored state of the console that can be recalled — essential in theatre and corporate, used sparingly in live music. §7.3 · §7.4
ScreenSkills
The UK skills body for film, TV and games — runs bursaries and short courses relevant to broadcast and post-production sound. §21.2
SDI
Serial Digital Interface — the professional video connector standard: a locking BNC connector on a single coax that runs reliably over long distances, unlike HDMI. §9.13
Self-powered
A speaker with its amp and processing inside — needs mains and signal at every box (e.g. Meyer Sound). §6.23 · §6.1
Sends on Fader
Flips the faders to show every channel's send to one mix — the quick way to build a monitor mix. §7.8
Session (DiGiCo)
The whole show on a DiGiCo — channels, I/O, routing. Snapshots change values within it. §7.11
Show file
A saved snapshot of every setting on a digital console. §7.4
Sidechain
Using one signal's level to control processing applied to a different signal — a gate keyed from another input, or a music bed "ducked" under a voiceover. §16.10
Slapback
A single short echo (tens of milliseconds) used to thicken a vocal. §16.8
SLink
Allen & Heath's link to stage boxes and expanders (GX, DX, AB, AR). §7.12
SM
Stage Manager — runs what happens on stage during a show, calls cues in theatre. §1.3
Smaart
Rational Acoustics' dual-channel FFT software — the industry standard for live system measurement and tuning. Paid, with widely respected training. §8.3 · §6.8 · §18.6
Soundscape ecology
The study of sound in an environment, including biological sounds, weather and human activity. It is a possible later bridge between audio skills and conservation, not a replacement for live-audio foundations. Outside work
Soundvision
L-Acoustics' prediction software. §6.21 · §6.7
Speaker level
The high-voltage signal between a power amp and a passive speaker. §2.2
speakON
The locking connector standard used for speaker-level connections. §3.3 · §12.13
Spectrogram
A visual plot of frequency against time, with amplitude shown by brightness or colour — the standard way to read a recording in both system measurement and bioacoustics. §8.3 · §18.15
Splay angle
The angle between boxes in a line array, set from prediction software — tight at the top for throw, wider at the bottom. §6.6 · §6.7
Split mode
On a full DM7, running the desk as two independent consoles — e.g. FOH and monitors. §7.8
Squelch
The threshold below which a wireless receiver mutes rather than passing noise. Set as low as reliably possible. §5.8 · §5.9
Stagebox
The numbered socket box at the stage end of a multicore or digital snake, where stage sources are plugged in. §3.5 · §11.2
Stage plan
A top-down drawing of stage positions, wedges, DIs and power. §1.5
Statutory Sick Pay
Paid from the first day off sick since April 2026, with no minimum earnings; employees only. §13.15
Stock label
The company’s own identifier on an item, case or cable—often a barcode, asset number or label scheme. Record it exactly when logging a fault or matching an equipment-system export to physical stock. Equipment-system handover
SWL / WLL
Safe Working Load / Working Load Limit — the rated maximum for a piece of lifting equipment. Never exceeded, never guessed. §6.5 · §13.3
System check
Proving the PA itself works before any input is checked. §11.1 · §19.6
System check (d&b)
A d&b amp test that compares each connected loudspeaker against a known-good reference, flagging a missing box, dead driver or bad cable. §6.16 · §6.18
Talkback
A mic at the console routed to monitors, comms or the stage. §7.6
T-coil
The coil inside a hearing aid that picks up a hearing loop's magnetic field directly, bypassing room noise. §6.11
Tech spec
The client's or artist's contractual technical requirements. §1.5
Tie line
Permanent cabling installed in a venue between fixed points, so you can get a signal across the building without running your own cable. §9.5 · §1.3
Titan (Avolites)
Avolites' lighting control software — runs on their consoles and, via Titan PC Suite, on a normal PC. §9.8 · §9.10 · §17.4
Titan PC Suite
The free software package that puts the full Titan environment (plus the Simulator) on a Windows PC. §9.9 · §18.10
Touch tour
A pre-show session letting blind and partially-sighted patrons handle set pieces and props to build a mental picture before the performance. §6.13
Transfer function
A measurement comparing a system's output (the mic) with its input (the reference), showing magnitude, phase and coherence. §8.11 · §6.8
TRS / TS / TRRS (jack)
Quarter-inch/mini jack connector types by contact count: TS carries one unbalanced signal, TRS either one balanced signal or two unbalanced, TRRS a stereo-plus-mic headset signal. The same connector can mean different things depending on the socket. §3.2
Universe (DMX)
One DMX line's worth of 512 control channels. §9.7 · §9.10
User Defined Keys
Programmable keys on Yamaha desks (on the DM7, four banks of twelve on the 7" screen). §7.8
Vectorworks
The industry-standard CAD package for live events — stage plans, rigging plots and full system drawings. §8.8
Virtual soundcheck
Building a mix from a recorded multitrack instead of a live band on stage. §14.3
Vision mixer (switcher)
The video department's console — takes several sources in and decides what's live (PGM) versus queued up next (PVW). §9.14
VLAN
A virtual network inside a switch — keeps audio, control or lighting apart on shared hardware. §8.6
VocalEyes
A UK charity providing audio description and other access services to arts venues. §6.13
VU-NET
Martin Audio's software for controlling and monitoring its amps and systems. §6.23
Wedge
A floor monitor speaker angled up at a performer. §15.2 · §15.6
Wink (R1)
The R1 function that flashes an amp's display so you can find it in the rack. §6.22
Wireless Workbench
Shure's free RF coordination and monitoring software — plan frequencies, then watch a live system for interference during a show. §5.5 · §18.14

The duty covering anywhere a fall could cause injury — ladders, MEWPs, rigging above head height. §13.1 · §13.3

UK employment law setting the 48-hour average working week (commonly opted out of in events), plus statutory rest breaks and rest periods. §13.15

Audiokinetic's game-audio middleware, roughly FMOD's main alternative. Its free 101/201/301 courses are a genuinely respected way into game audio without a games degree. §21.5
