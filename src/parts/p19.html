19.13 Pocket reference

Referenced from §0.2. The numbers and scripts you actually want mid-job, condensed onto one printable page — not a replacement for the full book, just the bit that's worth having in your back pocket. Every figure here is explained properly elsewhere; follow the section links if you need the reasoning, not just the number.

XLR pinout

Pin 1 = screen/earth, pin 2 = hot (+), pin 3 = cold (−). "One is ground, two is hot." (§2.3)

speakON NL4 / NLT4

Pins 1± = first channel (d&b: top, or LF). Pins 2± = second channel (d&b: sub, or HF). A 2-core cable carries pins 1 only. Mute, push in, twist until it clicks. (§3.12 · §6.17)

Which d&b amp?

If the box's setup isn't in the amp's list, that amp can't drive it. Not on a D20: J-Series, M2, SL-Series. Not on a D40: GSL, SL-SUB, J-Series, M2. (§6.20)

Signal levels

Mic level ≈ 1–10 mV. Line level ≈ 0.3–1.5 V (+4 dBu pro / −10 dBV consumer). (§2.2)

Gain staging target (digital desk)

Average around −18 dBFS, peaking to about −10 dBFS. Regularly touching red = gained too hot. (§2.5)

dB rules of thumb

  • +3 dB = double the power, barely audible
  • +6 dB = double the voltage, clearly audible
  • +10 dB ≈ twice as loud to a human ear
  • Double the distance = −6 dB (inverse square law)

Phantom power, fast

Condenser mic / active DI = needs it. Dynamic mic = safe either way. Ribbon mic = can be damaged by it — check before switching a bank on. Always mute before switching. (§2.6)

Frequency crib

  • 20–250 Hz — sub-bass/bass (boomy if too much)
  • 250 Hz–2 kHz — low mid/mid (boxy/honky if too much)
  • 2–8 kHz — presence (harsh/sibilant if too much)
  • 8–20 kHz — air (brittle/hissy if too much)

(§2.11)

The first sixty seconds of any fault

  1. One thing, or everything?
  2. What changed right before it broke?
  3. Check cheap & obvious first — mute, fader, gain, connector, power
  4. Then follow the chain
  5. Narrate the logic, even just in your head

(§12.8, full walkthroughs from §12.9)

Delay & distance

Sound ≈ 344 m/s → 2.9 ms per metre. 10 m ≈ 29 ms · 30 m ≈ 87 ms. Ask the chat: “delay for 25 m”. (§6.8)

d&b amp lights

Green = signal present. Yellow GR = limiting (ease off). Red = muted (touchscreen amps) or overload (D6/D12). Error text → write it down, tell the system owner. (§6.17)

Radio licences (UK)

Ch 38 606.5–613.5 MHz — shared licence (£75/yr online). Ch 70 863.1–864.9 MHz — licence-free. Anything else needs a site licence. (§5.12)

Dante, fast

One clock leader · same sample rate everywhere · EEE off on switches · primary and secondary never linked · save the routing preset. (§8.9)

Noise at work

Daily: 80 dB(A) lower action · 85 upper action (protection required) · 87 limit. Peaks: 135 / 137 / 140 dB(C). (§13.5)

Your hours

48 h/week averaged over 17 weeks (opt-out allowed) · 11 h rest between days · 20-min break over 6 h · 5.6 weeks' holiday · sick pay from day one. (§13.15)
